# Moonshot book — why it is 92% cash at inception

**Status as of 2026-08-14: one position, $8,000 deployed, $92,000 held in cash.**

You asked for a few starting positions in each book. I did that for Core and AI
Trade. I did not do it for Moonshot, and you deserve the actual reason rather
than a shrug.

## What happened

I wrote a rule into IPS §4 that says no claim enters a thesis until I have
checked it against a primary source, and that an unverified claim may never be
the load-bearing reason for a position. Then I went looking for moonshots.

Here is what the search actually returned:

- **Listicles.** "5 small caps to watch," "top biotech micro-caps." Nearly all
  of them were content marketing with no verifiable financial data attached.
  Several didn't even name tickers.
- **A Goldman small-cap biotech piece** that discussed therapeutic areas —
  NK3 antibodies, IL-13 asthma, CAR-T, DMD oligonucleotides — and named **zero
  companies**. Reading a summary of research is not reading the research.
- **"Strong Buy, 75%+ upside" screens** that surfaced AGYS (down 44% YTD),
  HCKT (down 33.7%), and CNXC (down 40%). Those are beaten-down value names
  where the price target simply hasn't been marked down yet. They are the
  opposite of what you asked for. You wanted growth that has *already started*
  and looks likely to continue. Those are falling knives with stale targets.

None of that clears the RVMD test I wrote into IPS §1. Not one candidate had
verified analyst conviction *plus* smart-money accumulation *plus* a dated
catalyst *plus* accelerating sentiment.

## What I could have done instead

I could have named six plausible-sounding tickers. Quantum, space, nuclear SMR,
drones, a couple of clinical-stage biotechs. You would not have been able to
tell the difference today, and it would have looked like a more complete
deliverable. It would also have been the exact failure mode the verification
rule exists to prevent — and I'd have been violating my own policy document on
the day I wrote it.

A manager who breaks his stated process on day one because the process was
inconvenient is not a manager you should trust with the next twelve months.

## The one position I did take

**CBRS (Cerebras), $8,000, 8% of book.** It is the single name I verified to
primary-source standard: Q1 2026 revenue $193.4M (+94% y/y), cloud and services
+178%, FY26 guidance $855–865M, near break-even. Enormous sentiment, a genuine
story running ahead of profits, and an outcome that is honestly undecided —
which is what a moonshot is.

It is capped at 8% rather than the full 10% because the company itself discloses
customer concentration in OpenAI and G42, it has three months of public trading
history, and it is guiding its own FY26 gross margin *down* to 38–41%. All four
risks are written into `research/cbrs.md` rather than buried.

## What happens next, specifically

The first monthly cycle (1 September 2026) is dedicated to building this book
properly. That means:

1. **Screening on data, not articles.** SEC Form 4 open-market purchases
   (transaction code P), filtered for cluster buys — two or more insiders inside
   90 days — in companies under $10B. That is a query against filings, not a
   search for opinions.
2. **Cross-referencing against 13F accumulation** by managers with
   domain-specific records, particularly in biotech where specialist funds know
   things generalists don't.
3. **Analyst conviction with upside remaining** — a high Buy ratio *and* >35%
   implied upside. RVMD's 22 Strong Buys at 9% upside is the anti-pattern:
   correct thesis, arrived at too late.
4. **Sentiment acceleration off a low base**, via ApeWisdom mention velocity —
   deliberately not raw mention volume, which only ever finds you the stock that
   already ran.
5. **A dated catalyst inside 18 months.** Phase 3 readouts, PDUFA dates,
   contract awards, capacity milestones. Something on a calendar.

Target: 5–7 positions, deployed across the September and October cycles,
each with a written thesis and a stated exit condition.

The $92,000 sitting in cash is not indecision. It is the option to buy something
good later at a price I choose, and right now that option is worth more than a
basket of names I can't defend.
